As part of the Capacity Building and Competency Development component of the ILASPP (Integrated Land Administration and Spatial Planning Project), the Geospatial Information Agency (BIG) held the *Workshop on Using the Piksel Platform for Cloud-Based Satellite Imagery Processing* on Monday, November 23, 2026, at Menara Peninsula Hotel, Jakarta.

The workshop was attended by 46 participants from eight ministries and agencies: the National Research and Innovation Agency (BRIN), the Ministry of Marine Affairs and Fisheries (KKP), the Ministry of Forestry, the Ministry of Agriculture, the Ministry of Environment, the Ministry of Agrarian Affairs and Spatial Planning/National Land Agency (ATR/BPN), the National Disaster Management Agency (BNPB), and BIG.

The workshop was opened by BIG's Director of Mapping, Dr. Gatot Pramono. In his remarks, he expressed hope that the Piksel Platform will be used to its full potential to support the duties and functions of each ministry and agency. By using cloud-based geospatial data processing, Piksel is expected to widen access to remote sensing data and technology, and to encourage the use of geospatial information in planning, monitoring, and policy-making.

During the workshop, participants were introduced to the Piksel Platform, including its technology architecture, the satellite imagery available, and the data processing capabilities it provides. The material also included demonstrations of several examples of how Piksel can be used for different geospatial analysis needs. This session showed how remote sensing and cloud computing can produce information that is relevant to many development sectors.

Beyond the theory, participants also took part in a hands-on session using Piksel Sandbox, which runs on Jupyter Notebook. In this session, participants were introduced to the steps of satellite imagery processing and built analysis modules themselves. This practice-based approach gave participants the chance to learn the data processing workflow, explore the platform's capabilities, and understand how it could be developed to fit the needs of their own institutions.

The participants' enthusiasm showed in the discussions and feedback shared during the workshop. One participant suggested holding a similar event over two days, so there is enough time to explore the features and capabilities of the Piksel Platform in more depth. This input will be an important consideration in planning the next capacity building programs.

Through this activity, BIG hopes to build closer collaboration with ministries and agencies in using the Piksel Platform, and to encourage wider use of geospatial technology in support of national development.
