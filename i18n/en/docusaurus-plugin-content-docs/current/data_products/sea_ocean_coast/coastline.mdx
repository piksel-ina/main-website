## About

Indonesia has a highly dynamic coastal zone, with more than 60% of the population living within a 50 km radius of the coastline. This coastline continuously faces diverse challenges such as extreme weather, climate change, sea-level rise, and rapid coastal development. A thorough understanding of how the coastline responds to these pressures is crucial for supporting sustainable coastal management from social, environmental, and economic perspectives.

Ina Coastline is a geospatial product built on the Piksel platform that maps Indonesia's shoreline position at near-mean sea level on an annual basis since 1987. By integrating historical Landsat satellite imagery and tidal modelling, the product generates annual shoreline data and detailed rate-of-change information. This enables quantitative observation of abrasion and accretion dynamics across various coastal landforms — beaches, sandbars, river mouths, and tidal flats — over time.

Through this dataset, annual shoreline change trends can be analysed at both local and national scales, facilitating historical mapping of coastal change patterns and supporting periodic data updates.

> **Note:** The **Ina Coastline** product maps shoreline positions based on near-mean sea level conditions. Therefore, all uses of the term **shoreline** in this document refer to the shoreline at mean sea level conditions, unless otherwise stated. This dataset was developed to support coastal dynamics analysis and monitoring. It is not an official product formally established and published by the Geospatial Information Agency of Indonesia (BIG), and therefore cannot be used as a legal reference.

## Applications

- Monitoring and mapping abrasion and accretion rates along Indonesia's coastline quantitatively.
- Assessing the impact of local and regional coastal management policies based on historical trend analysis.
- Modelling shoreline response and sensitivity to coastal dynamic drivers, such as extreme weather, sea-level rise, and rapid coastal development.
- Providing a historical database for geomorphological studies to understand the patterns, processes, and causes of shoreline change over time.

## Technical Information

This product consists of **three groups of vector layers** visualised through the Piksel platform. To visualise each layer, select the **View on Map** menu to open the Piksel interactive map service, then select the Coastline Layer and add the desired layer to the map.

### Annual Shoreline Layer — _shorelines_annual_

Annual shoreline vectors representing the median (most representative) shoreline position for each year since 1987. Shoreline positions are estimated at near-mean sea level (_Mean Sea Level_, MSL) used as the 0-metre elevation reference.

Shorelines displayed as dashed lines indicate a low *confidence level* for that shoreline position.

<figure>
  <img src={useBaseUrl('/img/coastline/layer-shorelines.png')} alt="Annual Shoreline Layer" />
  <figcaption>Figure 1. Annual shorelines from Ina Coastline.</figcaption>
</figure>

### Rate of Change Points Layer — _rates_of_change_

A point dataset presenting coastal change rates interactively at 30 m intervals along Indonesia's non-rocky coastline. The most recent annual shoreline is used as the *baseline* for rate-of-change measurements.

On the Ina Coastline interactive map, points are only displayed at locations meeting the following criteria:

1. Statistically significant rate of change (_p-value_ ≤ 0.01; see `sig_time` below)
2. Good data quality (_certainty_ = "good")

Each point presents the annual rate of change in metres per year (see `rate_time`), along with uncertainty estimates at the 95% confidence interval (see `se_time`).

All detailed attributes at rate-of-change points can be accessed by clicking on a labelled point on the map.

### Coastal Change Hotspot Layer — _hotspots_zoom_

Consists of three point-based data layers that summarise the level of coastal change using an aggregation radius of 1 km, 5 km, and 10 km along the coastline. These layers are specifically designed to facilitate mapping, visualisation, and identification of coastal dynamics at regional and national scales.

## Processing Workflow

import WorkflowSteps, { WorkflowStep } from '@site/src/components/Docs/WorkflowSteps';

<WorkflowSteps>
  <WorkflowStep label="LOAD DATA" phase="input">
    Load the full Landsat 5, 7, 8, and 9 archive for the study area using Open Data Cube (ODC) or STAC API. Processing prioritises Tier 1 (T1) imagery. Tier 2 (T2) is used as a supplementary fallback if T1 scene count is insufficient.
  </WorkflowStep>
  <WorkflowStep label="WATER INDEXING" phase="water">
    Calculate a per-pixel water index to separate water surfaces from land. Four configurable indices are available: **MNDWI** `(green − swir16) / (green + swir16)`, **NDWI** `(green − nir08) / (green + nir08)`, **Combined (CWI)** — average of MNDWI and NDWI, and **MNDWI-NIR** — a modified MNDWI variant using the NIR band, optimised for tropical coastal conditions in Indonesia.
  </WorkflowStep>
  <WorkflowStep label="TIDAL MODELLING" phase="tidal">
    Estimate tidal height at each pixel using an _Ensemble Tidal Modelling_ approach combining 10 global tidal models (EOT20, FES2012/2014/2022, TPXO8/9/10, GOT4.10/5.5/5.6). For Indonesian waters, **INATIDES** is additionally integrated — a regional tidal model calibrated specifically for the complex tidal characteristics of the Indonesian archipelago.
  </WorkflowStep>
  <WorkflowStep label="TIDE INTERPOLATION" phase="tidal">
    Interpolate tidal estimates from low spatial resolution to full resolution (30 m), so each image pixel has a precise and consistent tidal height value at each satellite acquisition time.
  </WorkflowStep>
  <WorkflowStep label="TIDE MASKING" phase="tidal">
    Filter and discard all observations outside the 50% tidal range centred at mean sea level (0 m _above mean sea level_). Only scenes at mid-tidal conditions are retained for shoreline analysis.
  </WorkflowStep>
  <WorkflowStep label="ANNUAL COMPOSITE" phase="composite">
    Combine all filtered observations into annual median composites from 1987 to 2025. Pixels with fewer than 5 clean observations are automatically gap-filled using a three-year median composite to maintain spatial data completeness.
  </WorkflowStep>
  <WorkflowStep label="COASTAL MASKING" phase="extraction">
    Apply morphological masking (_coastal mask_) to restrict analysis to valid coastal zones only. This step integrates geomorphology and land modification layers to exclude non-coastal areas and man-made structures from the shoreline extraction process.
  </WorkflowStep>
  <WorkflowStep label="SHORELINE EXTRACTION" phase="extraction">
    Extract waterline positions at a defined index threshold using the _subpixel waterline extraction_ method (Bishop-Taylor et al., 2019). This method produces shorelines with sub-pixel precision — accuracy of less than 30 metres.
  </WorkflowStep>
  <WorkflowStep label="CHANGE ANALYSIS" phase="analysis">
    Calculate coastal change rates at every 30-metre interval along the non-rocky coastline using linear regression of annual shoreline position against time. Produces derived statistics including rate of change (m/yr), significance level (_p-value_), _Standard Error_ (SE), SCE (_Shoreline Change Envelope_), NSM (_Net Shoreline Movement_), and the year of maximum and minimum position.
  </WorkflowStep>
</WorkflowSteps>

## Tidal Modelling

The Ina Coastline processing workflow supports two configurable tidal modelling approaches suited to the characteristics of the study area:

- **Single Model** — uses one selected tidal model directly. This approach offers faster computation and is ideal for areas with relatively simple tidal characteristics.
- **Ensemble Model** — combines multiple global tidal models in parallel, then weights each model's contribution based on its correlation with tidal inundation patterns from satellite observations and altimetry measurements. This method produces more reliable sea-level estimates in areas with complex tidal dynamics.

Tidal modelling is implemented using the _eo-tides_ Python library, which integrates remote sensing data with advanced tidal modelling functionality from the _pyTMD_ module.

### Supported Global Tidal Models

- Empirical Ocean Tide Model (EOT20)
- Finite Element Solution tide models (FES2012, FES2014, FES2022)
- TOPEX/POSEIDON global tide models (TPXO8, TPXO9, TPXO10)
- Global Ocean Tide models (GOT4.10, GOT5.5, GOT5.6)
- Hamburg direct data Assimilation Methods for Tides (HAMTIDE11)
- Technical University of Denmark tide model (DTU23)

### Regional Model: INATIDES

In addition to the global models above, Ina Coastline specifically integrates **INATIDES** — a regional tidal model calibrated and optimised for the hydrodynamic characteristics of Indonesian archipelagic waters. INATIDES is registered into the processing workflow using the `extra_databases` mechanism in the _eo-tides_ library. This flexible mechanism allows custom models to be used directly, either as a single model or as part of the ensemble, without modifying the core code.

## Notes and Limitations

### Annual Shorelines

- Annual shorelines from Ina Coastline present the median or most representative/dominant shoreline position throughout the year, corrected to a consistent tidal height at Mean Sea Level (0 m _above mean sea level_). This data is designed for long-term trends and does not reflect short-term coastal fluctuations such as daily tidal movements, seasonal dynamics, or brief impacts from individual storms or extreme waves.

### Rate of Change Points

- Rate of change point data presents the magnitude of spatial trends, but does not automatically identify the primary cause of change. In areas with rapid coastal development, construction of channels or waterways may be detected as abrasion hotspots. Conversely, reclamation, port construction, or jetty structures may be detected as accretion hotspots. Rate of change interpretations must therefore be evaluated alongside annual shoreline data and comparative satellite imagery.
- Rate of change point accuracy may decrease in coastal environments with rapid non-linear change, such as complex river mouth deposits. In these areas, users are advised to perform direct visual verification on the annual shoreline data layer used.

### Data Quality Issues

- Annual shoreline accuracy may decrease in areas with large tidal ranges and very gently sloping tidal flats. In these areas, small errors in tidal modelling can cause significant horizontal shifts in shoreline position.
- In some densely populated or urban coastal areas, spectral reflectance from bright white building rooftops near the coast may be inadvertently classified as water surface, causing the shoreline to shift landward from its true position.
- In aquaculture pond (_aquaculture pond_) areas in coastal zones, the boundary between pond water and land may be read as a shoreline by the extraction algorithm, resulting in noise in shoreline position within those pond areas.
- Shoreline data for 1991 and 1992 are affected by atmospheric aerosol disturbance from the 1991 eruption of Mount Pinatubo in the Philippines. The dispersal of ash columns and volcanic gas in the stratosphere affected the quality of Landsat spectral reflectance, so data over those two years must be interpreted with greater care.

## Quality Flags

All Ina Coastline data passes through an automatic screening process to detect potential data quality issues. Each feature identified as having limitations is flagged via the `certainty` attribute column, and visualised specifically on the interactive map using dashed lines or white points.

### Annual Shoreline Layer — _shorelines_annual_

- **aerosol issues**: Shoreline position accuracy for that year is affected by atmospheric aerosol disturbance from the 1991 eruption of Mount Pinatubo. This is likely in areas traversed by the dispersal of volcanic gas columns and particles in the stratosphere.
- **insufficient data**: Shoreline accuracy is limited due to minimal good-quality or cloud-free satellite observations at that location within a one-year period.
- **unstable data**: The shoreline is at a location with high data fluctuation. This instability may be triggered by tidal model deviation, the presence of very gently sloping tidal mudflats or sandbars, or high cloud interference in satellite imagery.

### Rate of Change Points Layer — _rates_of_change_

- **insufficient observations**: The amount of good-quality annual shoreline data is insufficient (< 25 years) to calculate a reliable coastal change trend.
- **likely rocky coastline**: The location is identified as a cliff or rocky coastline. Rates of change in this area may be less accurate due to topographic shadow interference on dark terrain.
- **extreme value (> 50 m)**: The location has a detected extreme rate of change (> 50 metres per year), and must be interpreted carefully alongside other supporting data.
- **high angular variability**: The rate of change may be less precise due to very high angular movement variability from the reference point to each annual shoreline.
- **baseline outlier**: The annual shoreline used as the _baseline_ (most recent condition) is detected as an _outlier_, potentially causing inaccurate rate of change calculations at that location.

### Coastal Change Hotspot Layer — _hotspots_zoom_

- **insufficient points**: The number of valid rate of change points within a 1 km / 5 km / 10 km radius around this location is too few to calculate a reliable regional rate of change estimate.
