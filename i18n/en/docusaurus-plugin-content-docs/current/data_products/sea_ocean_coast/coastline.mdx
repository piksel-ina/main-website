## About

Indonesia has a highly dynamic coastline, with over 60% of the population living within 50 km of the coast. This coastline faces diverse pressures including extreme weather, climate change, sea-level rise, and coastal development. Understanding how the coastline responds to these pressures is critical for coastal zone management from social, environmental, and economic perspectives.

Ina Coastline is a derived product of the Piksel platform that maps the position of Indonesia's coastline at mean sea level for every year since 1987. The product combines Landsat satellite data with tidal modelling to generate annual shorelines and detailed rates of change, enabling quantitative observation of coastal erosion and accretion on beaches, sandspits, river mouths, and tidal flats over time.

This dataset enables annual analysis of coastal erosion and accretion trends at local and national scales, and supports historical mapping and periodic updating of coastal change patterns.

> **Note:** The **Ina Coastline** product maps shoreline positions based on **_mean sea level (MSL)_**. Therefore, the term **shoreline** in this document refers to the shoreline at **_mean sea level (MSL)_** conditions, unless otherwise stated.

## Applications

- Monitoring and mapping coastal erosion and accretion rates along Indonesia's coastline.
- Assessing the impact of local and regional coastal management based on historical change.
- Modelling shoreline response to change drivers, including extreme weather events, sea-level rise, or coastal development.
- Supporting geomorphological studies on how and why coastlines change over time.

## Technical Information

This product consists of **three groups of vector data layers** visualised on an interactive map.

### Annual Shoreline Layer — _shorelines_annual_

Annual shoreline vectors representing the median or "most representative" position of the shoreline at approximately 0 m Above Mean Sea Level (AMSL) for each year since 1987 (see Figure 1).

Dashed shorelines indicate low confidence.

  <img src={useBaseUrl('/img/coastline/layer-shorelines.png')} alt="Annual Shoreline Layer" />
  <figcaption>Figure 1. Annual shorelines from Ina Coastline visualised on the interactive map.</figcaption>
</figure>

### Rate of Change Points Layer — _rates_of_change_

A point dataset providing robust coastal change rates for every 30 m interval along Indonesia's non-rocky coastline (see Figure 2). The most recent annual shoreline is used as the baseline for rate-of-change measurements.

On the Ina Coastline interactive map, points are only displayed for locations with statistically significant rates of change (_p-value_ ≤ 0.01; see `sig_time` below) and good data quality (_certainty_ = "good"). Each point displays the annual rate of change (in metres per year; see `rate_time`), along with uncertainty estimates (95% confidence interval; see `se_time`).

### Coastal Change Hotspot Layer — _hotspots_zoom_

Three point layers summarising coastal change within a 1 km, 5 km, and 10 km moving window along the coastline (see Figure 3). These layers are useful for visualising coastal change patterns at regional or national scale.

## Processing Workflow

import WorkflowSteps, { WorkflowStep } from '@site/src/components/Docs/WorkflowSteps';

<WorkflowSteps>
  <WorkflowStep label="LOAD DATA" phase="input">
    Load the full Landsat 5, 7, 8, and 9 archive available for the study area using Open Data Cube or STAC API. Priority is given to Tier 1 (T1) scenes; Tier 2 (T2) is used as a fallback if scene count is insufficient.
  </WorkflowStep>
  <WorkflowStep label="WATER INDEX" phase="water">
    Calculate a per-pixel water index to distinguish water surfaces from land. Four configurable indices are available: **MNDWI** `(green − swir16) / (green + swir16)`, **NDWI** `(green − nir08) / (green + nir08)`, **Combined (CWI)** average of MNDWI and NDWI, and **MNDWI-NIR** — a modified MNDWI variant using the NIR band for tropical coastal conditions.
  </WorkflowStep>
  <WorkflowStep label="TIDAL MODELLING" phase="tidal">
    Estimate per-pixel tidal height using an Ensemble Tidal Modelling approach combining 10 global tidal models (EOT20, FES2012/2014/2022, TPXO8/9/10, GOT4.10/5.5/5.6). For Indonesian waters, **INATIDES** is additionally used — a regional tidal model calibrated specifically for the complex tidal characteristics of the Indonesian archipelago.
  </WorkflowStep>
  <WorkflowStep label="TIDE INTERPOLATION" phase="tidal">
    Interpolate tidal estimates from low resolution to full resolution (30 m) so that each pixel has a consistent tidal height value at every image acquisition time.
  </WorkflowStep>
  <WorkflowStep label="TIDE MASKING" phase="tidal">
    Mask and discard all observations outside the 50% tidal range centred at mean sea level (0 m AMSL). Only scenes within the mid-tidal range are retained for analysis.
  </WorkflowStep>
  <WorkflowStep label="ANNUAL COMPOSITE" phase="composite">
    Combine all masked observations into annual median composites from 1987 to 2025. Pixels with fewer than 5 clean observations are automatically replaced using a three-year median composite (_gapfill_) to maintain data completeness.
  </WorkflowStep>
  <WorkflowStep label="COASTAL MASKING" phase="extraction">
    Apply morphological masking to restrict analysis to valid coastal zones only. This step also integrates modification and geomorphology layers to exclude non-coastal areas and man-made features from shoreline extraction.
  </WorkflowStep>
  <WorkflowStep label="SHORELINE EXTRACTION" phase="extraction">
    Extract waterline positions at a defined index threshold using the subpixel waterline extraction method (Bishop-Taylor et al., 2019). This method produces shorelines with sub-pixel precision (sub-30 m).
  </WorkflowStep>
  <WorkflowStep label="CHANGE ANALYSIS" phase="analysis">
    Calculate coastal change rates at every 30 m along the non-rocky coastline using linear regression of annual shoreline position against time. Produces derived statistics: rate of change (m/yr), p-value, standard error, SCE, NSM, and the year of maximum and minimum position.
  </WorkflowStep>
</WorkflowSteps>

## Tidal Modelling

The Ina Coastline pipeline supports two configurable tidal modelling approaches. **Single model** uses one selected tidal model directly — faster and sufficient for areas with relatively simple tidal characteristics. **Ensemble** combines multiple global models in parallel, then weights each model's contribution per location based on correlation with satellite-observed tidal inundation patterns and altimetry measurements — producing more robust estimates in locations with complex tidal dynamics.

Tidal modelling is implemented using the _eo-tides_ Python package, which integrates remote sensing data with tidal modelling through _pyTMD_ functionality.

### Supported Global Models

- Empirical Ocean Tide Model (EOT20)
- Finite Element Solution tide models (FES2012, FES2014, FES2022)
- TOPEX/POSEIDON global tide models (TPXO8, TPXO9, TPXO10)
- Global Ocean Tide models (GOT4.10, GOT5.5, GOT5.6)
- Hamburg direct data Assimilation Methods for Tides (HAMTIDE11)
- Technical University of Denmark tide model (DTU23)

### Regional Model: INATIDES

In addition to the global models above, Ina Coastline integrates **INATIDES** — a regional tidal model calibrated specifically for Indonesian archipelagic waters. INATIDES is registered into the pipeline using the `extra_databases` mechanism in `eo-tides`, which allows custom models to be used either as a single model or as an ensemble member without modifying the core code.

## Notes and Limitations

### Annual Shorelines

- Annual shorelines from Ina Coastline summarise the median ("dominant") shoreline position throughout the year, corrected to a consistent tidal height (0 m AMSL). Annual shorelines **do not** reflect short-term coastal variability, such as shoreline position changes between high and low tide, seasonal effects, or brief impacts of individual storm events.

### Rate of Change Points

- Rate of change points **do not assign a cause of change**, and do not necessarily represent coastal erosion processes or sea-level rise. At locations undergoing rapid coastal development, construction of new inlets or marinas may appear as retreat hotspots, while port or jetty construction may appear as accretion hotspots. Rate of change points should be evaluated with reference to the underlying annual shorelines and external data or imagery sources.
- Rate of change points may be inaccurate or invalid within complex mouthbars, or other coastal environments undergoing rapid non-linear change over time. In these areas, it is advisable to visually inspect the underlying annual shoreline data when interpreting rates of change.

### Data Quality Issues

- Annual shorelines may be less accurate in areas with complex tidal dynamics or large tidal ranges, and on gently sloping intertidal flats where small errors in tidal modelling can cause large horizontal offsets in shoreline position.
- In some urban locations, the spectrum of bright white buildings situated near the coastline can inadvertently be confused with water, causing a landward offset from the true shoreline position.
- In aquaculture pond areas located in coastal zones, the boundary between pond water and land can be read as a shoreline by the extraction algorithm, resulting in noise in shoreline position within those pond areas.
- The 1991 and 1992 shorelines are currently affected by aerosol issues caused by the 1991 eruption of Mount Pinatubo. These shorelines should be interpreted with caution, particularly in areas close to the path of the stratospheric plume — the column of gases and volcanic particles injected into the stratosphere that can spread thousands of kilometres following wind currents.

## Quality Flags

Ina Coastline data is automatically screened to detect potential data quality issues. Each feature identified as problematic is flagged via the `certainty` column, and visualised with dashed lines or white points on the interactive map.

### Annual Shoreline Layer — _shorelines_annual_

- **aerosol issues**: Shoreline accuracy may be affected by aerosol issues caused by the 1991 eruption of Mount Pinatubo, particularly in areas close to the stratospheric plume path.
- **insufficient data**: Shoreline accuracy may be affected by limited good-quality satellite observations at this location.
- **unstable data**: Shoreline accuracy is affected by unstable data at this location. This may be caused by tidal model errors, the presence of gently sloping intertidal mudflats or sandbars, or noisy satellite imagery due to high cloud cover.

### Rate of Change Points Layer — _rates_of_change_

- **insufficient observations**: There are insufficient years of good-quality annual shoreline data (< 25 years) to calculate a reliable coastal change rate.
- **likely rocky coastline**: This shoreline is identified as rocky or a cliff. Rates of change may be less accurate due to noisy shoreline mapping caused by dark terrain shadows.
- **extreme value (> 50 m)**: This location has been identified as having an extreme coastal change rate (> 50 metres per year) and should be interpreted with caution.
- **high angular variability**: This rate of change is likely inaccurate due to high angular variability from this point to each annual shoreline.
- **baseline outlier**: The baseline annual shoreline (i.e. the most recent) is flagged as an outlier, potentially resulting in inaccurate rates of change at this location.

### Coastal Change Hotspot Layer — _hotspots_zoom_

- **insufficient points**: There are too few valid rate of change points within a 1 km / 5 km / 10 km radius around this location to calculate a reliable regional rate of change.
