## Background

Indonesia's coastline faces increasing pressure — erosion from waves, sedimentation, sea-level rise, land reclamation, and coastal development. More than 60% of Indonesia's population lives within 50 km of the coast, making coastline change monitoring critical for coastal zone management.

Conventional field surveys cannot cover the full length of Indonesia's coastline on a regular basis. Satellite-based analysis provides a solution that can consistently map change year by year across the entire territory.

## Approach

This analysis uses the Landsat imagery archive from the Piksel platform to extract annual shoreline positions since 1987. Each image scene is filtered using the **INATIDES** tidal model — so only imagery acquired when sea level is close to the mean (_mean sea level_) is used. The result is annual shorelines that are temporally comparable and can be used to calculate coastal change rates.

This method is adapted from **DEA Coastlines** (Geoscience Australia) and implemented for Indonesian conditions using the [Ina Coastline](/docs/data_products/sea_ocean_coast/coastline) product.

### Key Findings

- Consistent coastline change detected since 1987 along the study area
- Erosion and accretion rates calculated per 30 m along non-rocky coastline
- Areas with statistically significant change rates (p-value ≤ 0.01) can be identified and mapped
- Dashed shorelines indicate areas with low data quality requiring careful interpretation
