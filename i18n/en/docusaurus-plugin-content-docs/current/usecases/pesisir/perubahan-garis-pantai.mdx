## Background

Indonesia's coastline faces increasing pressure — coastal abrasion from waves, sedimentation, sea-level rise, land reclamation, and coastal development. More than 60% of Indonesia's population lives within a 50 km radius of the coast, making coastline change monitoring critical for coastal zone management.

Conventional field surveys cannot cover the full length of Indonesia's coastline on a regular basis. Satellite-based analysis provides a solution that can consistently map change year by year across the entire territory.

## Approach

This analysis uses the Landsat imagery archive from the Piksel platform to extract annual shoreline positions since 1987. Each image scene is filtered using the **INATIDES** tidal model — so only imagery acquired when sea level is close to the mean (_mean sea level_) is used. The result is annual shorelines that are temporally comparable and can be used to calculate coastal change rates.

This method is adapted from **DEA Coastlines** (Geoscience Australia) and implemented for Indonesian conditions using the [Ina Coastline](/docs/data_products/sea_ocean_coast/coastline) product.

## Analysis Results

Rates of change are calculated using linear regression of annual shoreline positions against time. Each observation point represents a 30-metre interval of the coastline — shoreline positions from 1987 to 2025 are plotted, and a best-fit trend line is drawn. The slope of that trend line gives the rate of change in metres per year: negative values indicate landward movement (abrasion) and positive values indicate seaward movement (accretion). Only points with good data quality (_certainty = good_) are used in the summary below.

- **63.9% of points show accretion** and **36% show abrasion**, with a median rate of change of +0.16 m/yr — indicating most of Java's coastline is relatively stable, with a slight aggregate tendency towards land gain.
- **59.9% of good-quality points** show statistically significant trends (_p-value_ ≤ 0.01), with 52,568 significant accretion points and 23,626 significant abrasion points.
- The rate of change for 90% of points falls between **−2.4 and +7.4 m/yr**, with extreme values up to −101 m/yr and +97 m/yr at specific locations requiring field verification.
- The shoreline positional variability over 38 years (_Shoreline Change Envelope_) has a median of **42.9 metres** — meaning the shoreline on average moved that far between its furthest seaward and furthest landward positions.
- Of 1,960 hotspots with a 1 km aggregation radius, **59 points record accretion ≥ 3 m/yr** and only **7 points record abrasion ≤ −3 m/yr**, indicating intense abrasion pressure remains localised to specific segments.
